How to make a logo construction grid in Figma

Outline the logo, draw a line along each key edge, and fit a circle to each true curve. Keep only the lines that explain the shape.

Base grid or construction grid

They get mixed up, but they do different jobs.

  • Base grid. Comes first. You sketch the logo on it, so sizes and angles stay consistent.
  • Construction grid. Comes after. It shows the lines, circles and alignments in the finished logo.

You can use one without the other. A logo drawn freely can still have a clear construction.

Pick a base grid

GridGood for
SquareLetters, blocky marks and icons
TriangleFaceted marks and 60° angles
IsometricCubes and marks with depth
HexagonCells, badges and six-sided marks
CircularRings, arcs and round marks
RadialBlades, petals and anything that turns around a centre
Golden ratioTesting proportion, when it suits the idea

Draw the construction in Figma

  1. Duplicate the logo and flatten the copy, so you have one clean vector. Keep the original safe.
  2. Lower the copy’s opacity, or give it a thin stroke and no fill, then lock it.
  3. Draw a line along each important edge: top, bottom, left, right and the main joins. Run it past the logo on both sides.
  4. Add lines for the diagonals that repeat. Check that matching angles really match.
  5. Press O and hold Shift to draw a circle over each curve that is part of a circle. Match the radius and the centre.
  6. Give every line and circle the same thin stroke, in one colour.
  7. Group the lines and the circles, and name the groups.

If a circle almost fits, look closely. Either the curve is meant to be different, which is fine, or it needs a small correction.

Keep only what explains the logo

A construction grid is there to show decisions. If a line doesn’t touch an edge, a centre or a curve, take it out.

  • Show repeats. Equal stroke widths, shared angles and circles of the same size.
  • Show alignment. Where the mark meets the type, and where edges share a line.
  • Stay honest. Don’t add golden ratio circles to a logo that wasn’t drawn with them.
  • Trust your eye. Optical corrections break the grid on purpose. Leave them in.

Present the construction

  • One page, one logo. Give it room and keep the caption short.
  • Logo first. Show the finished logo before the lines, so people know what they’re looking at.
  • Say what it proves. “Five equal blades around one centre” says more than “construction”.

It fits in a concept deck to explain the idea, and in the brand guidelines as a reference.

Questions, answered.

Does every logo need a construction grid?

No. It helps when the shape has clear repeats, angles or curves. For an expressive or drawn logo, clear space and minimum size are more useful.

Should I draw the grid before or after the logo?

A base grid before, if it helps you sketch. The construction grid after, once the shape is final.

Do logos need to follow the golden ratio?

No. It’s one way to test proportion. Use it when it fits the idea, and leave it out when it doesn’t.

Can I make a construction grid from a PNG?

Not an accurate one. Draw the logo as a vector first, then build the construction from that.

What colour should construction lines be?

One colour that stands apart from the logo, with a thin stroke. Blue, grey or one of the brand’s colours all work.
