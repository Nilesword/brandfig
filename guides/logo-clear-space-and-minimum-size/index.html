How to set logo clear space and minimum size in Figma

Take a unit from the logo, such as its cap height, and keep that much space on every side. For minimum size, make the logo smaller until a detail fails, then go back up.

You hand over a logo, and the next time you see it there’s a web address pushed against its edge. Or it’s on a pen, with the type filled in. Two small rules prevent most of that.

What clear space is

Clear space is the empty area around a logo. Text, other logos, busy photo details and the page edge stay out of it. It’s a minimum, so more room is always fine. You’ll also see it called an exclusion zone or a safe area.

Pick a unit from the logo

Use a part of the logo as the unit, not a fixed number. It then scales with the logo, on a business card or on a billboard.

UnitGood for
Cap heightLogotypes, and logos with a mark and type
Width of the markCompact marks. Often a half or a quarter of it
Part of the logo’s heightLogos with nothing obvious to measure. Try a half or a quarter
One part of the markA dot, a blade or a stroke people can spot
PixelsOne fixed size only, such as an app header

One cap height on every side is a good place to start. Compare it with half and with two. Too little and the logo feels crowded. Too much and nobody follows the rule.

Mark clear space in Figma

  1. Duplicate the logo and work on the copy.
  2. Draw a square as tall as a flat capital in the logotype, such as H or E. This is your unit. Round letters overshoot, so don’t measure those.
  3. Place a copy of the square on each side, touching the outermost edge of the logo.
  4. Draw a rectangle around the outside of the squares. That’s the clear space.
  5. Give the area a light, flat fill and send it behind the logo.
  6. Label the unit, such as “x = cap height”.
  7. Group the marks and name the group Clear space.

Measure from the shape you can see. If the frame around the logo has padding, the space will come out too big.

Find the minimum size

Minimum size is the smallest the logo can go before it stops being clear. Test it instead of guessing.

  1. Place the logo at 200, 120, 80, 60 and 40 px wide.
  2. View them at 100% zoom on a normal screen and on a phone.
  3. Find the first one where a gap closes, a thin stroke fades or a word is hard to read.
  4. Set the minimum one step above it.
  5. Print the same row at real size in millimetres, and repeat.
  • Screen and print are separate. Give pixels for screen and millimetres for print.
  • Each version has its own limit. The mark can usually go smaller than the full logo.
  • Say what to measure. Write “24 px wide” or “24 px high”, not just 24 px.
  • Plan what comes next. Below the minimum, switch to the mark or a simpler version.
  • Ask about the material. Embroidery, engraving and vinyl have their own limits, so check with whoever makes it.

Write the rule

Keep it to two lines a client can follow:

  • Keep one cap height clear on every side of the logo.
  • Don’t use the full logo below 80 px wide on screen or 20 mm wide in print.

Use your own tested numbers. These only show the wording.

Questions, answered.

How much clear space should a logo have?

One cap height, or a quarter to a half of the mark’s width, is a common starting point. Test it on real pages and adjust.

What is the minimum size for a logo?

There’s no standard number. It depends on the thinnest stroke, the smallest gap and the type, so test each version on screen and on paper.

Is clear space the same on every side?

Usually, because it’s easier to follow. A logo with an uneven shape can have different space per side if the rule stays clear.

What is x in a clear space diagram?

The unit, taken from part of the logo. 2x means twice that part.

Does clear space apply to favicons and app icons?

Not in the same way. They have very little room, so set their padding separately.
