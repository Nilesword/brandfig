What to include in brand guidelines: a checklist

Cover the logo, colour and type, then show them working together. Add voice, photography and layouts when the brand has them.

A new marketing hire opens the file on their first day. They want to know which logo to use, which blue is right and which font goes in a headline. Good guidelines answer those in a minute, without a call to you.

The pages every brand needs

PageWhat it answers
Logo and versionsWhich logo do I use here?
Clear space and minimum sizeHow close and how small can it go?
Logo on backgroundsWhich version works on this colour?
Logo misuseWhat should I never do to it?
Colour paletteWhat are the exact values?
Typefaces and hierarchyWhich font, weight and size?
ExamplesWhat does it look like together?
Files and contactWhere are the files, and who do I ask?

Logo

  • Show each version large. Say when to use it in one line.
  • Set the space. Give clear space and minimum sizes for screen and print.
  • Show it on real backgrounds. White, dark and the brand’s colours.
  • Keep misuse short. Six likely mistakes are more useful than twenty unlikely ones.
  • Show construction if it explains something. Here’s how to draw a construction grid.

Colour

  • Give every colour a role. Primary, secondary, accent, neutral.
  • List the values. HEX, RGB and CMYK. Add a spot colour if the brand prints often.
  • Show the amounts. A palette with one dominant colour looks different from one with five equal ones.
  • Show text pairs that are easy to read. Check the contrast. WCAG AA asks for 4.5 to 1 for normal text and 3 to 1 for large text.

Type

  • Name the fonts and weights. Say where to get them.
  • Set roles. Headline, subhead, body and caption, with size and line height.
  • Pick a fallback. For email and slides, where the brand font may not load.

Add these when the brand has them

  • Purpose and values. Short, and in the client’s own words.
  • Voice. A few traits, with a line written the right way and the wrong way.
  • Photography. Subject, light and crop, with pictures to follow and to avoid.
  • Pattern, icons and shapes. How they’re made and where they go.
  • Stationery, social, packaging, website. Only what the brand will really use.
  • Motion. If the logo or the type moves.

How long should it be?

BrandLengthCover
New or very smallOne pageLogo, colours, type
Small business10 to 20 pagesThe pages every brand needs
Growing team20 to 40 pagesPlus voice, imagery and layouts
Several teams or markets40 or morePlus each channel and product

These are starting points. Leave a page out when it has nothing useful to say.

Write rules people can follow

  • Say what to do first. “Use the white logo on photos” is easier to follow than a list of bans.
  • One idea per page. A short line and a clear picture.
  • Give the reason when it isn’t obvious. People keep rules they understand.
  • Add a date. So everyone knows which version they have.

Questions, answered.

What is the most important part of brand guidelines?

The logo, colour and type pages. They answer the questions people ask most often.

Does a small business need brand guidelines?

Yes, but a short set. One page with the logo, colours and type is enough to start.

What’s the difference between a brand book and brand guidelines?

People use the terms for the same thing. When there’s a difference, a brand book covers the story and values, and guidelines cover how to use the design.

Should brand guidelines be a PDF or a website?

A PDF is easy to send and keep. A website suits a large team that updates often. Many brands begin with a PDF.

How often should brand guidelines be updated?

Whenever the logo, colours or type change, or when people keep asking the same question.
